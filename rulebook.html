Battlestar Galactica: 7-Player Consolidated Rulebook

House edition · Version 1.2 · October 2026

The base game plus Pegasus, Exodus and Daybreak, trimmed to the modules our group chose. When this book and a card disagree, the card wins. When this book and an official rulebook disagree, check the Decision Log first: some differences are deliberate. Everything else follows the official rulebooks, FAQ 2.1, and the fan Unofficial Combined Rulebook v2.0 (CR2.0), which merges all four rulebooks.

The Mutineer is in this edition. Version 1.2 adds the "You Are the Mutineer" Loyalty card, which is the official Daybreak setup for 7 players. Its rules are in §15.5. Appendix A says what to change if the group ever wants to play without it.

2. Overview and How to Win

BSG is a team game with hidden teams. Most players are human and want to reach Earth. Some are secretly Cylons trying to destroy humanity. One player is a known Cylon Leader whose true side depends on secret Motive cards. Each player's team comes from their Loyalty cards, and it can change when new cards arrive at the Sleeper Agent phase.

2.1 Human victory

The fleet must travel 10 distance, then jump one more time. That final jump ends the game in a human win, as long as no Cylon victory condition happened first. Distance comes from Destination cards and passed distance Missions.

2.2 Cylon victory

All Cylons win if any of these happens:

  • A resource runs out: food, fuel, morale or population is at 0 at the end of any player's turn. A resource can hit 0 and be raised again in the same turn without losing.
  • Centurion invasion: a centurion reaches the end of the Boarding Party track.
  • Galactica destroyed: 6 or more Galactica locations are damaged at the same time.
  • No characters left: a human is executed and there's no character left to choose. This is very unlikely with all expansions.

2.3 Cylon Leader victory

The Leader wins with whichever side wins, only if at game end they have (a) no more than one unrevealed Motive card, and (b) at least two revealed Motives whose allegiance matches the winning side. With two human and two Cylon Motives revealed, they win with either side. See §17.

3. Components and Pre-Game Sort

Do this before game night. It saves 20+ minutes at the table.

Put in the box (not used):

  • Kobol, New Caprica and Ionian Nebula Objective cards
  • New Caprica board, New Caprica Crisis deck, occupation forces tokens
  • Ally cards and tokens, Trauma tokens, Crossroads cards, alternate basestar damage tokens (Exodus)
  • Pegasus Treachery cards, Pegasus Cylon Locations overlay, Pegasus Infiltration card
  • Agenda cards (Hostile and Sympathetic)
  • Loyalty cards: You Are a Sympathizer, You Are a Sympathetic Cylon, all 8 Personal Goal cards
  • Base President title card; base and Pegasus Admiral title cards (use the Exodus Admiral card and the newest President card)
  • All Cylon Attack cards from the Crisis deck (19 across base, Pegasus and Daybreak) and both from the Super Crisis deck
  • Mining Asteroid Destination card
  • 2 of the 8 basic vipers
  • Cardboard basestar tokens (use plastic), centurion markers (use plastic centurions), Scar token
  • The original Investigative Committee Politics cards replaced by the 7 Exodus replacements

Shuffle together: all remaining Crisis cards from every box (including "CAG Chooses" crises), all Super Crises, all Destinations, all Quorum cards, each Skill type across boxes, and the Daybreak Treachery deck.

Deck counts after sorting (useful for checking): 14 Motive cards, 22 Mutiny cards, 8 Mission cards. The Loyalty cards we use are 18 You Are Not a Cylon, 5 Final Five, 7 You Are a Cylon and 1 You Are the Mutineer.

Table space: main board in the centre; Pegasus beside it; the Cylon Fleet board on the other side; Demetrius next to one of them. Leave room for the Rebel Basestar, which may arrive mid-game. The official books disagree on left and right, so use whatever layout fits the table.

4. Setup

Follow these steps in order.

1. Objective. Place the Earth Objective card face up next to the Destination deck.

2. Boards.

  • Main board in the centre. Set food 8, fuel 8, morale 10, population 12.
  • Place the Daybreak Colonial One overlay and Cylon Locations overlay, with the "Colonial One Destroyed" and "Hub Destroyed" sides face down.
  • Place the Pegasus board, the Cylon Fleet board (pursuit marker on Start) and the Demetrius board.
  • Set the Rebel Basestar board and allegiance marker aside until the Cylon Civil War mission calls for them.
  • Put the fleet marker on the Start space of the Jump Preparation track.

3. Ships and tokens.

  • Reserves: 4 basic vipers, 4 raptors, 1 assault raptor. The starting assault raptor can't be placed during setup. Other assault raptors stay beside the board until gained.
  • 4 Viper Mark VIIs in the Damaged Vipers box.
  • 2 vipers in the space area below Galactica and 2 civilian ships (drawn at random) behind Galactica.
  • 1 basestar and 3 raiders in front of Galactica.
  • Everything else (damage tokens face down, other ships, nukes, miracle tokens) goes beside the boards.

4. Cards.

  • Shuffle the Quorum, Crisis, Super Crisis, Mutiny, Destination and Mission decks.
  • Sort Skill cards into six decks by type: Politics, Leadership, Tactics, Piloting, Engineering and Treachery. Shuffle each.
  • Destiny deck: deal 2 cards of each of the six types, Treachery included (12 cards), and shuffle.

5. First player. Choose one at random and give them the current player token.

6. Choose characters. Starting with the first player and going clockwise:

  • Each player picks a character of whichever type (Political Leader, Military Leader or Pilot) is most plentiful among those still available. Support characters and the Cylon Leader may be picked at any time.
  • Alternate versions (Lee Adama, Tom Zarek, Helo, Baltar): once one version is taken, the other is unavailable. It still counts when working out which type is most plentiful.
  • Exactly one Cylon Leader, and one is required at 7 players. If several players want it, the first to pick gets it. If nobody has picked a Leader by the last player, that player must take one (a house clarification of the official requirement).
  • Each player takes their sheet, stand-up token, piloting token if any, and 1 miracle token, then places their token at the setup location on the sheet. Apollo may start in an assault raptor instead of a viper.

7. Titles.

  • President: the character highest on the President line of succession. They draw 1 Quorum card.
  • Admiral (Exodus Admiral card): the highest on the Admiral line. They take 2 nuke tokens; the third goes beside the board.
  • CAG: the highest on the CAG line.
  • A Cylon Leader can never hold a title (see §17).

8. Loyalty deck.

  • Set the You Are the Mutineer card aside. Separate the other Loyalty cards into a You Are a Cylon pile and a You Are Not a Cylon pile. The Not-a-Cylon pile is the 18 plain cards plus the 5 Final Five cards. Shuffle each pile.
  • Build the Loyalty deck: 2 You Are a Cylon + 11 You Are Not a Cylon + the Mutineer = 14 cards.
    • +1 Not-a-Cylon if someone chose the original Gaius Baltar (Political Leader). Not for the alternate (Support) Baltar.
    • +1 Not-a-Cylon if someone chose Sharon "Boomer" Valerii.
  • Return the unused Cylon cards to the box without looking. Keep the leftover Not-a-Cylon pile beside the Loyalty deck, clearly separate; executions draw from it.
  • Shuffle the Loyalty deck and deal 1 card face down to each non-Leader player. Original Baltar gets 2. The Cylon Leader gets none.
  • If someone is dealt the Mutineer, they reveal it at once, draw another Loyalty card, draw 1 Mutiny card and hand over any titles they hold (§15.5).

Why 14 cards? The Daybreak chart for 7 players is 2 Cylon + 10 Not-a-Cylon + the Mutineer. The 11th Not-a-Cylon card is Exodus's rule: when using Exodus, add 1. Six players receive Loyalty cards (6 at setup, 6 at the Sleeper phase) and the Mutineer's extra draw makes 13, so one card is never dealt. That leaves about a 1-in-7 chance that one Cylon card sits out.

9. Motives. Deal the Cylon Leader 2 Motive cards. Give them the Daybreak Infiltration reference card.

10. Read. Everyone takes 30 seconds to read their Loyalty or Motive cards, even if the card is short, so reading time gives nothing away.

11. Starting Skill cards. Every player except the first player draws 3 Skill cards from their skill set, in any combination the set allows. The first player draws at the start of their first turn. The Cylon Leader draws 2, except Athena, who starts Infiltrating and draws 3.

5. Core Concepts and Secrecy

5.1 "Human players" and "Cylon players"

  • Players means everyone.
  • Cylon players means revealed Cylons, plus a Cylon Leader who is not Infiltrating.
  • Human players means everyone else, including hidden Cylons and an Infiltrating Cylon Leader.

5.2 Secrecy rules

These rules keep hidden Cylons from being found by mechanical deduction.

  • Golden rule: you may only use "polar opposites", high/low or a lot/a little. Never "medium", "pretty high" or exact numbers. The one exception: you may say "a medium amount" when you're playing several cards into a check.
  • Skill checks: never state the exact strength, colour or type of cards you hold or play, and never say afterwards which cards were yours. You may say how many cards you're playing, because that's public.
  • Asking about abilities: you may ask if anyone holds a specific card ("does anyone have Strategic Planning?"). Answering is optional. Don't recite your hand.
  • Loyalty cards: if you look at someone's Loyalty cards, you may only say "human" or "Cylon". Never mention card text or how many Cylon cards you saw. The owner is told which card was looked at.
  • Final Five: you can't say you have one or describe its text. You may hint, for example "you really don't want to look at my cards", whether or not it's true.
  • Top of a deck: when you see an upcoming Crisis or Destination card, you may only call it "good" or "bad".
  • Civilian ships: if you see a civilian ship's face, you may only say "a lot" or "a little".
  • Revealed Cylons still keep their hands and Super Crises secret.
  • Communications: civilian ships looked at with Communications are looked at secretly; don't show them.
  • Open information: the number of cards in each hand, each Skill deck, the Quorum deck and the Destiny deck; the top card of each discard pile (but don't count discard piles).
  • Accusations are always allowed and have no rules effect.

6. Characters, Miracles and Titles

6.1 Character sheets

Each sheet shows the character's type, two positive abilities, one negative ability, a once-per-game (miracle) ability, a skill set and a setup location. Abilities starting "Action:" use your action for the turn. Other abilities trigger when their text says. If several players want to use abilities at the same moment, the current player decides the order.

6.2 Miracle tokens

  • Using your character's once-per-game ability costs your miracle token. No token, no miracle.
  • You can hold only one token. If you gain one after spending yours, you can use the ability again.
  • Alternate Baltar (Broadcast) can hold up to 3 tokens and must spend 3 to use Broadcast. He can be chosen to gain a token while he has fewer than 3.
  • When told to "choose a player to gain a miracle token", you must choose someone without one. If everyone eligible already has one, nobody gains one. A Cylon Leader without a token can be chosen even when not Infiltrating.
  • Revealing as a Cylon discards your token, and revealed Cylons can't gain tokens. Cylon Leaders keep theirs when an Infiltration ends.
  • A character chosen after an execution does not get a new token.

6.3 The President

  • Holds the Quorum hand. Quorum cards give actions only the President can use. Draw more with the President title card or the Quorum Chamber location.
  • Quorum hand limit: 10 at the end of any turn.
  • Keeps the title while in the Brig.

6.4 The Admiral

  • Uses the Exodus Admiral title card. Starts with 2 nukes and may never hold more than 3.
  • Chooses the destination at each jump (§13).
  • Loses the title if sent to the Brig; it goes to the highest in line who isn't in the Brig.

6.5 The CAG (Commander, Air Group)

  • Uses the CAG title card's ability, and makes the "CAG Chooses" decisions on crises.
  • Places civilian ships whenever an effect places one without naming the area. The CAG must pick an area without a civilian ship if possible; if every area has one, any area.
  • Loses the title if sent to the Brig, like the Admiral.

6.6 Succession (all three titles)

  • A title passes to the highest in that line if the holder is executed or revealed as a Cylon. When a character is executed, the replacement character is included in this check.
  • The Admiral and CAG titles also pass if the holder is sent to the Brig. They go to the highest in line not in the Brig. If every human is in the Brig, the highest-ranked prisoner holds the title until someone gets out; the first one out takes it immediately.
  • A title-holder who leaves the Brig does not automatically get the title back.
  • A new character chosen after an execution does not take a title just for ranking higher.
  • An Infiltrating Cylon Leader can never be President, Admiral or CAG.
  • A player who receives the Mutineer card gives each title they hold to the next in line, not counting themselves (§15.5). After that they can gain titles normally.

7. The Game Turn

Turns go clockwise. Each turn has seven steps.

Step 1: Receive Skills

  • Draw the Skill cards listed on your sheet, from your own skill set, no matter how many you already hold.
  • Multi-skills (two-coloured entries): decide how many to draw from each type, totalling the listed number.
  • In Sickbay or on the Resurrection Ship: draw only 1 card.
  • Revealed Cylons: draw 2 cards of any types, each from a different deck.
  • Infiltrating Cylon Leader: draw 1 extra card from your set (3 total). It may be a type you've already drawn.
  • If a Skill deck and its discard pile are both empty, you can't draw that type until cards are discarded.

Step 2: Movement

  • You may move your character to any location you're allowed to use. Humans can't enter Cylon locations; revealed Cylons can only move among Cylon locations.
  • Changing ships costs 1 Skill card. That covers Galactica, Colonial One, Pegasus, Demetrius and the Rebel Basestar, and leaving a viper for a ship.
  • Nobody may move into a hazardous location (yellow-striped border: Brig, Sickbay, Resurrection Ship, Hub Destroyed). You only go there when an effect sends you.
  • Movement abilities replace moving; you do one or the other. Only one per turn, and only on your own turn: not when moved by someone else's card, such as Executive Order. They can be used from the Brig. An effect that prohibits a type of action (e.g. "no Piloting actions") also prohibits Movement abilities of that type.
  • Piloting a viper: move the viper one area (Mark VII: up to two), or discard 1 Skill card to return to any ship location (the viper goes to the Reserves and the piloting token comes off).
  • Landing a viper is a move, never an action. Do it in your Movement step, or with a move granted by Executive Order or State of Emergency.

Step 3: Action

Choose one:

  • Activate your location's action (not if it's damaged).
  • Play a Skill card's Action.
  • Use a character sheet Action.
  • Activate your piloted viper: move, attack or escort (§11.1).
  • Use a Title, Quorum or Mutiny card Action.
  • Reveal a You Are a Cylon card and resolve its Action (not allowed from the Brig).
  • Infiltrating Cylon Leader: return to the Resurrection Ship (§17.4).
  • Do nothing.

You may take any action from the Brig. The Brig only limits movement and how many cards you put into skill checks.

Step 4: Crisis

Draw the top Crisis card, read it aloud and resolve it (§10).

  • Skip this step if: you're a revealed Cylon, you're in the Brig, or anyone activated the Demetrius Bridge this turn (§14).

Step 5: Activate Cylon Ships

Resolve the Cylon ship icon(s) on the Crisis card (§11.6). Revealed Cylons skip this step. With no crisis drawn, there's nothing to activate.

Step 6: Prepare for Jump

If the crisis shows the prepare for jump icon, advance the fleet marker one space. Reaching Auto Jump jumps the fleet (§13). This step still happens on a revealed Cylon's turn if they resolved a crisis through Caprica. On the Mutineer's turn, resolving a jump icon in this step makes them draw a Mutiny card (§15.5).

Step 7: Discard

At the end of every turn, anyone holding more than 10 Skill cards discards down to 10, and the President discards down to 10 Quorum cards. Super Crises don't count. Then discard the turn's Crisis card and pass the current player token to the left.

8. Movement and Locations

Each location's action is printed on the board or overlay; read it from there. This section covers the rules that apply around those locations.

8.1 The ships and their locations

  • Galactica: FTL Control, Weapons Control, Command, Admiral's Quarters, Hangar Deck, Armory, Research Lab, Communications, plus the hazardous Brig and Sickbay.
  • Colonial One: use the Daybreak overlay locations. If Colonial One is destroyed (e.g. by the Bomb on Colonial 1 Super Crisis), flip the overlay and send everyone on it to Sickbay.
  • Pegasus: Pegasus CIC, Airlock, Main Batteries, Engine Room.
  • Demetrius (§14) and, if it arrives, the Rebel Basestar (§14.5).
  • Cylon locations: use the Daybreak Cylon Locations overlay, plus the Basestar Bridge on the Cylon Fleet board.

8.2 Board skill checks

  • Admiral's Quarters: accuse any character. Difficulty 7, Leadership + Tactics. Pass sends the accused to the Brig.
  • Brig: try to escape. Difficulty 7, Politics + Tactics. Pass lets you move to any Galactica location.
  • Airlock (Pegasus): used to execute a character, as printed on the board. On the Airlock, Treachery cards count as positive strength.
  • Everyone may contribute to these checks under the normal skill check rules.

8.3 The Brig

  • You can't move out except by passing the Brig check or through an effect that specifically mentions the Brig: Presidential Pardon (Quorum), Make a Deal (Mutiny), and the Dishonest Tactics and Rebuild Trust crises.
  • You skip the Crisis step.
  • You may play only 1 Skill card into each skill check.
  • You still draw Skill cards normally and may take actions and use Movement abilities.
  • A Cylon who reveals in the Brig doesn't get the Action on their Loyalty card.
  • An effect can't choose someone who's already in the Brig, a revealed Cylon, or Helo before he's on the board.
  • If you're in the Brig and an effect sends you to Sickbay, you stay in the Brig.

8.4 Sickbay

  • You draw only 1 Skill card on your turn.
  • You move out freely during Movement.
  • The same targeting limits as the Brig apply.
  • If an Executive Order moves you out of Sickbay before your turn starts, you draw your full skill set that turn.

8.5 Damaged locations

  • A damaged location's action can't be used until it's repaired. You may still stand there.
  • A location being damaged sends everyone in it to Sickbay.
  • Damaged FTL Control doesn't stop the fleet marker advancing or the fleet auto-jumping.

8.6 Cylon locations (overlay)

  • Resurrection Ship is hazardous. Revealed and executed Cylons go there, and a Cylon there draws only 1 Skill card.
  • Hub Destroyed: if an effect flips the overlay, pick up any tokens on it, flip it, and put them back in the same locations. Anyone on the Resurrection Ship goes to Hub Destroyed, which is then used for anything that mentions the Resurrection Ship.
  • Caprica: on the Daybreak overlay, using Caprica does not skip the Prepare for Jump step.
  • Cylon Fleet location: "activate all ships of one type" works exactly like resolving that activation icon, so it can place ships on the Cylon Fleet board and advance the pursuit marker. Activating heavy raiders this way advances centurions on the Boarding Party track even if no heavy raiders or basestars are on the board. "Launch 2 raiders and 1 heavy raider from each basestar" only affects basestars on the main board and never adds ships to the Cylon Fleet board.
  • Human Fleet: this is where a Cylon Leader starts an Infiltration (§17.4).
  • Basestar Bridge (Cylon Fleet board): only revealed Cylons may go there. Moving between it and the overlay's Cylon locations costs no card. Activating it resolves two different of its four abilities, one at a time; the same ability can't be used twice in one activation. If it tells you to place a basestar and both basestars are already on a board, nothing happens.

9. Skill Cards and Skill Checks

9.1 Skill types

Type Colour Typical use
Politics Yellow Morale; drawing outside your skill set. The most common crisis skill.
Leadership Green Moving other players; granting actions. The second most common crisis skill.
Tactics Purple Die-roll bonuses; scouting destinations.
Piloting Red Rerolling enemy attacks; extra attacks.
Engineering Blue Repairing vipers and locations.
Treachery Brown Sabotage; mainly for Cylons.

Normal Skill decks range from strength 0 to 6 (average about 2.2). Treachery has twelve 0s and the rest 3–5.

9.2 Destiny deck

  • 2 cards of each of the six types, including Treachery (12 cards).
  • Two Destiny cards go into every skill check.
  • When the Destiny deck runs out, the current player builds a new one the same way.
  • Tip: appoint a Skill Supervisor to sort discards, reshuffle empty decks and rebuild the Destiny deck.

9.3 Skill check procedure

  1. Read. The current player reads the whole card or location aloud. Players discuss within the secrecy rules. Any required choice (current player, President, Admiral or CAG) is made now. A choice without a pass/fail effect means you just follow its instructions instead of making a check.

  2. Destiny. Deal 2 Destiny cards face down to start the pile.

  3. Play skills. Starting left of the current player and ending with the current player, each player gets one chance to add cards face down:

    • Brig: 1 card.
    • Revealed Cylon: 1 card, and it's optional.
    • Infiltrating Cylon Leader: up to 2 (1 if in the Brig).
    • Anyone else: any number.

    Card text is ignored except for Skill Check Abilities.

  4. Shuffle and divide. Shuffle the pile and deal it face up into matching cards (colours listed on the check) and non-matching cards.

  5. Abilities.

    • First, the current player resolves every Skill Check Ability (icon beside the strength) in any order. Duplicates of the same ability resolve once.
    • Then, if the crisis has a Consequence result and at least one card with the Skill Check Ability icon was played, resolve the Consequence. This happens whether the check passes or fails.
    • Then, if the check is Reckless, resolve the Reckless procedure (§9.6).

  6. Total. Matching strength minus non-matching strength.

  7. Result. If the total is at least the difficulty, apply pass. Otherwise apply the partial pass if the total meets that number, or else fail. Discard all the cards to their piles.

9.4 Skill Check Abilities

  • These resolve only when the card is played into a check, whoever played it: human, Cylon or Destiny.
  • If Dogfight or Quick Thinking removes a card before its ability resolves, that ability doesn't happen. An ability that already resolved stands.

9.5 Treachery

  • Treachery counts as negative strength in checks unless the card says otherwise (the Airlock counts it as positive).
  • Revealed Cylons may draw Treachery; most humans can't draw it unless an effect allows.
  • Treachery cards reading "When a player chooses to discard this card, he must draw 1 Mutiny Card":
    • Discarding one by choice (to a crisis, for the hand limit, or to change ships) draws a Mutiny card.
    • Random discards don't trigger it, and neither does playing the card into a check.
    • You can draw at most one Mutiny card per turn this way.

9.6 Reckless skill checks

  • One Reckless card may be played per check, before any cards are added. The check is then Reckless.
  • After all Skill Check Abilities are resolved, flip the top Treachery card:
    • Strength above 0: discard it with no effect.
    • Strength 0: flip the next one too. Resolve both cards' Skill Check Abilities, even ones already resolved this check, then discard both.
  • These cards never count toward strength.
  • Restore Order can't be played on a Reckless check, and a check can't be made Reckless after Restore Order.

9.7 Movement abilities on Skill cards

See §7 step 2.

10. Crises

10.1 Crisis types

  • Skill check crises show a difficulty and coloured skill boxes. Some let the current player, President, Admiral or CAG choose between the check and an alternative.
  • Events have no check; just follow the text. Some include a choice.
  • With a choice, you may always pick either option, even one you can't fully carry out, unless the card forbids it.

10.2 Card icons

  • Bottom left: Cylon ship activation (§11.6).
  • Bottom right: Prepare for Jump.

10.3 Super Crises

  • A Cylon receives one on revealing (not if revealed by execution).
  • Played by activating Caprica.
  • Resolved like a crisis, but immune to abilities that affect crises or skill checks.

10.4 Crisis mix

Just for strategy: about 50% of crises hit morale, 23% food, 16% population and 11% fuel. 65% have a jump icon.

11. Combat

The fleet is in combat whenever any Cylon ship is on the main board.

11.1 Activating vipers

You usually activate unmanned vipers through Command. Each viper may be activated several times in a turn, but never one another player is piloting. Each activation is one of:

  • Launch: Reserves to a space area with a viper launch icon.
  • Move: to an adjacent area. Mark VII: up to two areas. Vipers go around Galactica, never over it.
  • Attack: a Cylon ship in the same area (roll on the Attack Table, §11.7).
  • Escort: shuffle a civilian ship in the viper's area back into the unused civilian pile.

11.2 Piloting

  • Characters with Piloting in their skill set can pilot. Use the Hangar Deck action to launch a viper with yourself in it: put your piloting token under it and your stand-up on your sheet. If none are in the Reserves, you may pull an unmanned viper back from a space area to use.
  • While piloting, your Action may be activating your own viper.
  • If your viper is damaged or destroyed, you go to Sickbay.
  • If you're sent to the Brig or Sickbay while piloting, your viper returns to the Reserves.
  • On a jump: pilots return to the Hangar Deck and their vipers to the Reserves, except assault raptors (§11.4).

11.3 Viper Mark VIIs

  • They start damaged and must be repaired before use.
  • They move up to two areas, and are damaged on 6–7 and destroyed on 8.
  • They count as vipers for every effect; the current player chooses which kind to use.

11.4 Assault Raptors

  • Start with 1 in the Reserves. Gain more through Raptor Specialist (Skill), Weapons Armed (Mutiny) or Trial by Fire (Crisis). If an effect says to destroy a raptor to build one, the raptor must come from the Reserves.
  • They count as vipers for all effects, never as raptors.
  • They're never damaged, only destroyed, on a roll of 7–8. If you must pick a viper to damage and pick an assault raptor, it's destroyed.
  • On a jump, a piloted assault raptor may stay in its space area. The current player may keep unmanned ones in place too.

11.5 Raptors

Raptors never fight. Cards may tell you to risk a raptor: there must be one in the Reserves, you roll, and on a result below the target the risked raptor is destroyed and you get no reward.

11.6 Cylon ship activation

  • Resolve icons left to right. Activate ships one space area at a time, in the current player's order. Each ship activates at most once per turn.
  • Each ship does the first thing on its list it can do.
  • With the Cylon Fleet board, an activation that would do nothing puts a ship on the Cylon Fleet board instead (§12).

Activate Raiders:

  1. Attack a viper in its area. An unmanned viper is attacked first; if two are piloted, the current player chooses.
  2. Otherwise destroy a civilian ship in its area; the current player chooses which.
  3. Otherwise move one area toward the nearest civilian ship. On a tie, move clockwise.
  4. Otherwise, if no civilian ships are on the board, attack Galactica.
  5. If no raiders are on the board, each basestar launches 2 raiders.
  6. If no raiders and no basestars are on the board: Cylon Fleet (§12).

Launch Raiders:

  1. Each basestar on the main board launches 3 raiders.
  2. If no basestars are on the main board: Cylon Fleet.

Exception: if the only basestar on the board has a Disabled Hangar token, no new basestar is placed and the pursuit marker doesn't move.

Activate Heavy Raiders and Centurions:

  1. Every centurion on the Boarding Party track advances one space. This happens even when a revealed Cylon causes the activation.
  2. Heavy raiders move toward the nearest area with a viper launch icon. A heavy raider that starts its move in such an area boards Galactica: remove it and put a centurion on the track's start. If all 4 centurions are already out, the heavy raider stays.
  3. If no heavy raiders are on the board, each basestar launches 1.
  4. If no heavy raiders, centurions or basestars are on the main board: Cylon Fleet.

Activate Basestars:

  1. Each basestar attacks: roll to damage Galactica or Pegasus (§11.8).
  2. If no basestars are on the main board: Cylon Fleet.

Exception: if the only basestar has a Disabled Weapons token, nothing is placed and the pursuit marker doesn't move.

Centurions:

  • Several centurions can share a space on the Boarding Party track; they don't push each other.
  • Destroy them with the Armory action.
  • A centurion reaching the end of the Boarding Party track means the Cylons win.

11.8 Damage

  • Cylon ships: a destroyed Cylon ship leaves the board but can come back later.
  • Vipers: damaged ones go to the Damaged Vipers box until repaired. Destroyed vipers, assault raptors and raptors leave the game.
  • Civilian ships: a destroyed ship is flipped face up, the fleet loses what it shows, and it's removed from the game. There are 12: six show 1 population, two show 2 population, one shows population + fuel, one shows population + morale, and two show nothing.
  • Galactica and Pegasus:
    • When Galactica is damaged, the current player draws a random damage token and may draw a Pegasus token instead.
    • Resource tokens: lose the resource shown, then remove the token.
    • Location tokens: place the token on the location; everyone there goes to Sickbay. A repaired location returns its token to the pool.
    • Pegasus destroyed: all 4 Pegasus locations damaged at once. Send everyone on Pegasus to Sickbay and remove the board.
    • Galactica destroyed: 6 Galactica locations damaged at once. The Cylons win.
  • Basestars: draw a random base-game basestar damage token and place it on the basestar.
    • Critical Hit: counts as 2 damage.
    • Disabled Hangar: the basestar can't launch.
    • Disabled Weapons: the basestar can't attack Galactica.
    • Structural Damage: +2 to attacks against it.
    • 3 damage destroys a basestar; its tokens go back into the pool.
    • An effect that says "destroy a basestar" removes it with no tokens drawn.
    • All basestar damage is cleared when the fleet jumps.

12. The Cylon Fleet Board

The Cylon ships chase the fleet all game instead of vanishing at each jump.

12.1 The board

  • Six Cylon space areas match the six areas around Galactica: 7–8 is in front of Galactica, 4 and 2 are the launch-icon side, 5–6 and 3 are the other side, and 1 is behind.
  • The Pursuit track measures how close a full attack is.
  • The Basestar Bridge is a location for revealed Cylons (§8.6).

12.2 Placing ships on the Cylon Fleet board

  • When an activation finds nothing to act on (the "Cylon Fleet" steps in §11.6), place one ship of the listed type on this board. The current player rolls a d8 for which area; roll modifiers such as Strategic Planning or Anders's Longshot apply. Then advance the pursuit marker one space.
  • Out of ships: if no ship of that type is left to place, find the highest-numbered Cylon space area holding one. All ships of every type in that area jump to the matching area on the main board.

12.3 The Pursuit track

  • Civilian ship spaces: when the marker lands on a space showing one civilian ship, the CAG places 1 civilian ship on the main board. On the two-ship space, the CAG places 2, one at a time. The CAG should keep them away from where Cylon ships are waiting.
  • Auto Attack: when the marker reaches it, every ship on the Cylon Fleet board jumps to its matching area on the main board. Then reset the marker to Start.

12.4 Placing ships on the main board

When a card or icon places Cylon ships on the main board (e.g. Cylon Ambush, Launch Raiders):

  1. Take them from ships on neither board.
  2. If that's not enough, take them from the Cylon Fleet board, lowest-numbered area first.
  3. If still not enough, the current player chooses which aren't placed.

12.5 Jumping with the Cylon Fleet

  • Cylon ships on the main board move to the matching Cylon space areas. They aren't removed.
  • Civilian ships stay where they are. They leave only when destroyed, escorted or removed by an effect.
  • Centurions on the Boarding Party track stay.

13. Jumping the Fleet

13.1 Two ways to jump

  • Auto Jump: the fleet marker reaches the Auto Jump space.
  • FTL Control: usable when the marker is on a blue space. Roll a d8; on 6 or less, lose population equal to the number on the marker's current space. Then jump.

13.2 Jump procedure

  1. Vipers: vipers in space return to the Reserves and pilots go to the Hangar Deck. Assault raptors may stay (§11.4). Civilian ships stay.
  2. Cylon ships: main-board Cylon ships move to the matching Cylon Fleet areas. Remove all basestar damage tokens. Centurions stay on their track.
  3. Mission card: clear the Active Mission space (§14.3).
  4. Choose destination: the Admiral draws 2 Destination cards, picks one, and puts the other on the bottom of the deck without showing it.
  5. Resolve it: follow its instructions, then place it face up next to the Earth Objective card. Its distance number adds to the total.
  6. Objective check: at 4+ distance for the first time, run the Sleeper Agent phase (§15.4). At 10+ distance, the next jump is the final one (§20).
  7. Reset: move the fleet marker to Start.

Destinations average about 1.9 distance: 31% are 1, 47% are 2 and 22% are 3.

14. The Search for Home: Earth, Demetrius and Missions

14.1 Earth Objective

  • Travel 10 distance. The next jump after that ends the game: don't draw a Destination; go to the end game (§20).
  • Distance comes from Destination cards and passed distance Missions.

14.2 Demetrius

  • Moving onto or off Demetrius costs 1 Skill card, the same as any ship change.
  • Its locations can't be damaged.
  • Other Demetrius locations are as printed on the board.

14.3 Missions (the Bridge)

  • Starting one: a human activates the Bridge on Demetrius and flips the top Mission card face up onto the Active Mission space.
  • One per jump: while a card is on Active Mission, nobody can use the Bridge. It stays until the next jump.
  • No crisis that turn: if anyone activates the Bridge during a turn, the current player doesn't draw a Crisis card that turn. That also means no crisis-driven Cylon ship activation and no crisis jump icon.
  • Resolving it: the mission is a skill check resolved immediately. Pass or fail, apply the result printed on the card.
  • No boosts: abilities and cards that affect crises or skill checks don't apply to missions. That includes Adama's Command Authority, Chief's Blind Devotion, Athena's For Love, Investigative Committee, Restore Order, Declare Emergency and Support the People. Skill Check Abilities on cards played into a mission aren't resolved.
  • Limits still apply: Brig, Cylon and similar limits on how many cards a player can contribute still count.
  • Distance missions show a distance number at the bottom. Pass: the distance counts immediately. Fail: turn the card face down on the Active Mission space; it gives no distance.
  • Clearing the space at a jump:
    • Face-up card with a distance number: place it next to the Earth card.
    • Face-up card without one: put it in the Mission discard pile.
    • Face-down (failed) card: shuffle it back into the Mission deck.
  • An empty Mission deck is rebuilt by shuffling the discard pile.

Mission cards we know (8 total; read the rest when drawn):

  • Search for Home: +2 distance on a pass.
  • Attack on the Colony: on a pass, remove all basestars from the main board, then remove 1 basestar from the game.
  • The Red Stripes: on a pass, remove all heavy raiders and centurions from the main board, then remove 2 of each from the game.
  • Cylon Civil War: brings in the Rebel Basestar (§14.5).

14.4 Worked example

The fleet has 8 distance. A human uses the Bridge. Search for Home is flipped and passed, and counts +2 immediately, making 10. That player draws no crisis this turn. The next jump ends the game.

14.5 The Rebel Basestar

  • It enters play when Cylon Civil War resolves. Place the board and its allegiance marker.
  • Pass: human side up. Humans treat it as another ship; moving onto or off it costs 1 Skill card. Cylons can't enter it or use its locations.
  • Fail: Cylon side up. Cylon players may discard 1 Skill card to move between any Cylon overlay location and the Rebel Basestar, and between the Basestar Bridge and the Rebel Basestar. Humans can't enter.
  • Its locations can't be damaged.
  • Raider Bay: you may activate only the 2 raiders or 4 vipers placed by that activation. If Apollo uses Alert Viper Pilot to jump into one of those vipers, the activating player can't activate that viper.

15. Loyalty, the Final Five and the Sleeper Agent Phase

15.1 Loyalty basics

  • Keep Loyalty cards face down; you may look at your own anytime.
  • If you hold even one You Are a Cylon card, you're a Cylon, and your other cards don't matter.
  • When an effect looks at one card of a player who has several, the card is chosen at random.

15.2 The Final Five

  • A Final Five card reads You Are Not a Cylon, and for winning it's exactly a Not-a-Cylon card. At game end it does nothing.
  • If you also hold You Are a Cylon, you're simply a Cylon.
  • The text only triggers when another player looks at it through an effect (e.g. Baltar's Cylon Detector), or when it's revealed by execution.
  • When looked at:
    • The looker must reveal it and hand it back.
    • The owner resolves its text, turns it face down, and shuffles it back with their other Loyalty cards.
    • If several Final Five cards are seen at once, the current player orders their effects, even if one causes an execution.
  • When revealed by execution: resolve its text, then remove it from the game. A non-Cylon Final Five character does not resurrect; the execution resolves as a human (§19).
  • Revealed Cylons aren't affected by Final Five text.
  • You may never state that you hold one. Hinting is allowed (§5.2).

15.3 Your starting cards

Each non-Leader player has 1 Loyalty card (original Baltar has 2, and the Mutineer has 1 besides the Mutineer card). 13 of the deck's 14 cards are dealt by the end of the Sleeper phase, so most games have 2 hidden Cylons and about 1 game in 7 has only 1. There are about 2–3 Final Five cards in the game.

15.4 Sleeper Agent phase (at 4+ distance)

  1. Deal 1 Loyalty card to every player except the Cylon Leader, including revealed Cylons. Boomer's sheet gives her 2. The Cylon Leader draws 2 Motive cards instead.
  2. Cylon players look at their new face-down Loyalty cards and give all of them to one human player of their choice.
  3. Mutineer: a human player who receives the Mutineer card (hidden Cylons included) reveals it at once and draws another Loyalty card. A revealed Cylon who is dealt it doesn't reveal it; the human they pass their cards to reveals it as if it had been dealt to them.
  4. Mutineer still hidden? If the Mutineer card hasn't been revealed by now, in this phase or earlier, the current player chooses a human player to draw 1 more Loyalty card. If that card is the Mutineer, they reveal it but don't draw the usual extra card.
  5. Everyone reads for 30 seconds.

A player may become a Cylon now. They can stay hidden or reveal later.

15.5 The Mutineer

The You Are the Mutineer card is a Loyalty card that is never kept secret.

  • Team: it counts as You Are Not a Cylon. The Mutineer wins with the humans unless they also hold a You Are a Cylon card.
  • Receiving it face down (at setup or the Sleeper phase): reveal it immediately and draw another Loyalty card. That card stays hidden and could be a Cylon card.
  • Whenever anyone receives it: draw 1 Mutiny card, and give each title you hold to the next in line, not counting yourself. After that you gain and lose titles normally.
  • More Mutiny cards: on the Mutineer's own turn, if one or more prepare for jump icons are resolved in the Prepare for Jump step, the Mutineer draws 1 Mutiny card. This includes a jump-track advance from Pegasus's Engine Room, and it's one card per step however many icons resolve. A turn with no crisis (the Bridge was used, or the Mutineer is in the Brig) has no icon and no draw.
  • A higher limit: the Mutineer holds up to 2 Mutiny cards. They go to the Brig on drawing a third, and keep 2 there (§18.3).
  • If the Mutineer reveals as a Cylon, they give the card face up to a human of their choice, at any distance. That player draws 1 Mutiny card and gives up their titles, but doesn't draw an extra Loyalty card.
  • If the Mutineer is executed: see §19.1.

16. Revealed Cylons

16.1 Revealing

  • A hidden Cylon reveals only as their Action, by flipping a You Are a Cylon card and resolving its Action.
  • From the Brig, you may reveal but don't get the Action.

16.2 Revealing procedure

  1. Discard: down to 3 Skill cards; all Mutiny cards; your miracle token; any "keep in play" Quorum cards.
  2. Titles pass by succession.
  3. Resurrect: move to the Resurrection Ship, or Hub Destroyed if the overlay is flipped. A piloted viper returns to the Reserves.
  4. Super Crisis: draw 1 at random. Not if you were revealed by execution.
  5. Extra Loyalty cards: if the fleet has traveled 6 or less, give all your other face-down Loyalty cards to one human. At 7+ distance, you can't hand them off.
  6. Mutineer: if you were the Mutineer, give the Mutineer card face up to a human of your choice, whatever the distance. They draw 1 Mutiny card and give up their titles (§15.5).
  7. End turn: if it's your turn, it ends immediately, and you don't draw a crisis.

16.3 A revealed Cylon's turn

  • Draw 2 Skill cards of any types, each from a different deck.
  • Move to any Cylon location, or use a Movement ability.
  • Act: use your location's action only. No Skill card, Quorum or character actions; your character sheet is ignored.
  • No Crisis step and no Activate Cylon Ships step.
  • Prepare for Jump still happens if you resolved a crisis through Caprica.
  • Discard to 10 at the end of each turn, as usual.

16.4 Revealed Cylon rules

  • You may ignore negative effects of crises and skill checks, such as discards or being sent to the Brig or Sickbay, and crisis executions (unless the card names Cylons).
  • You can play 1 Skill card into each check, optionally. You can't use Skill card text abilities.
  • When you draw a crisis through Caprica and it poses a choice for the current player, you decide it.
  • You can't be sent to the Brig or Sickbay, and you can't be forced to discard.
  • You can't draw or play Mutiny cards.
  • You can't gain miracle tokens.
  • You can't be targeted by Executive Order or by Quorum cards.
  • You can't use the Actions on any unrevealed You Are a Cylon cards you still hold, though a Cylon location such as the Resurrection Ship may let you give them away.

17. Cylon Leaders

17.1 Basics

  • Exactly one Cylon Leader, and one is required at 7 players. Pegasus and Daybreak Leaders all use the Daybreak rules and Motive cards.
  • Everyone knows from the start that the Leader is a Cylon, but the Motives decide which side they're really on.
  • A Leader has a skill set and draws from it: 2 cards per turn (3 while Infiltrating). They draw 2 at setup; Athena draws 3.
  • All abilities on their sheet are always active, including the negative one. They may use sheet Actions instead of their location's action.
  • Otherwise they're treated as a revealed Cylon, except while Infiltrating.
  • They ignore effects that add a Not-a-Cylon card and draw a Loyalty card, and they never hold Loyalty cards.

17.2 Motive cards

  • The Leader gets 2 at setup and 2 more at the Sleeper phase.
  • Each card has an allegiance (human or Cylon) and a condition.
  • Reveal a Motive whenever its condition is currently true, even mid-action, mid-check or mid-crisis. A condition that was true earlier doesn't count.
  • At game end the Leader may reveal any Motives whose conditions are true in the final game state.
  • Revealing all 4 is possible.
  • Winning: at most 1 Motive left unrevealed and at least 2 revealed Motives matching the winning side. With 2 human and 2 Cylon Motives revealed, they win with either side.
  • Errata: A Justified Response reads "Reveal this card if the fleet marker is on a blue space of the Jump Preparation track and there are no raiders, heavy raiders or basestars on the main game board."

17.3 Mutiny and miracles

  • A Leader draws and uses Mutiny cards only while Infiltrating, and discards them when Infiltration ends.
  • Mutiny cards that make you "choose a player" can target an Infiltrating Leader.
  • A Leader keeps their miracle token when Infiltration ends.

17.4 Infiltrating

  • Start: activate the Human Fleet location and move to any Galactica location.
  • While Infiltrating the Leader is a human player:
    • Moves among human locations only.
    • Draws a Crisis card at the end of each turn.
    • Uses Skill card text.
    • Draws 3 Skill cards per turn.
    • Plays up to 2 cards per check (1 in the Brig).
  • Titles and Quorum cards: can't be President, Admiral or CAG, and can't receive Assign Vice President. May receive other Quorum cards, such as Assign Mission Specialist or Assign Arbitrator.
  • Ending it: as an Action, the Leader may return to the Resurrection Ship (or Hub Destroyed). From the Brig, they must first discard down to 3 Skill cards. Any return, including by execution, ends Infiltration: discard any given Quorum cards (without effect) and all Mutiny cards.
  • Infiltrating doesn't change allegiance; the Motives still decide who the Leader wins with.
  • Boomer and Athena can both be in one game. Athena's presence proves Boomer is a Cylon model, not which side Boomer is on.

18. Mutiny Cards and the Brig

18.1 How you get Mutiny cards

  • From effects that tell you to draw one: crises, locations such as the Press Room, Quorum cards such as Encourage Mutiny, and characters such as alternate Zarek.
  • The Mutineer draws one on receiving the Mutineer card, and on each of their own turns in which a jump icon is resolved (§15.5).
  • From choosing to discard certain Treachery cards (§9.5), or Dradis Contact or Bait. At most one Mutiny card per turn from these discards.

18.2 Using them

  • Keep them hidden.
  • Use one as your Action during the Action step, then discard it face up. An empty Mutiny deck is reshuffled from its discards.

18.3 The card limit

  • The moment you draw a second Mutiny card, you go to the Brig, unless the effect says otherwise (e.g. the Press Room, or alternate Zarek's Abuse Power). There's no end-of-turn check.
  • In the Brig you keep 1 Mutiny card. You can still draw and play them there; drawing a second in the Brig means discarding down to 1.
  • The Mutineer's limit is one higher: they go to the Brig on drawing a third card, and keep 2 there.

18.4 Who can't use them

  • Revealed Cylons never draw, play or get chosen for Mutiny cards.
  • A hidden Cylon who reveals discards all of theirs.
  • Cylon Leaders use them only while Infiltrating (§17.3).

18.5 Errata

  • Peaceful Resistance can be played from the Brig, even by the Admiral, but the player stays in the Brig.
  • Unauthorized Usage reads: "Action: Launch 1 nuke at a space area. Then remove this card from the game and discard all nuke tokens."
  • Violent Protest can be played while the President is in Sickbay or the Brig; if in the Brig, the President doesn't move.

19. Execution

Executions come from the Airlock, some crises, some cards, and Final Five text.

19.1 Execution procedure

  1. Discard all your Skill and Mutiny cards, your miracle token(s), and any Quorum cards played on your character (e.g. Assign Mission Specialist). Quorum cards in the President's hand are unaffected.

  2. Prove loyalty:

    • All Not-a-Cylon cards: reveal them all and go to step 3.
    • Any You Are a Cylon: reveal one (don't take its Action), give all your other face-down Loyalty cards to a human of your choice, and go to step 4. If you were the Mutineer, also pass the Mutineer card face up to a human of your choice, as when revealing (§15.5).
    • Cylon Leader: no cards to reveal; go to step 4.

    A Final Five card revealed now resolves its text and is then removed (§15.2).

  3. Human:

    • A. Lose 1 morale.
    • B. Your character leaves the game permanently.
    • C. Discard all your Loyalty cards. Keep the Mutineer card if you have it: your new character is the Mutineer, with the card face up.
    • D. Choose a new character of any type and place them at their setup location. If that location is unavailable, use Sickbay. You don't get a miracle token.
    • E. Add 1 card from the Not-a-Cylon pile to the Loyalty deck, shuffle, and draw 1 Loyalty card. If the executed character was Boomer and the Sleeper phase hasn't happened yet, draw 1 more.

  4. Cylon: move to the Resurrection Ship (or Hub Destroyed) and follow the revealed-Cylon procedure (§16.2), but draw no Super Crisis.

  5. End turn: if it was your turn, it ends, whatever you turned out to be.

19.2 Other execution rules

  • Titles change hands after the new character is chosen, so the new character can inherit.
  • No characters left: if a human is executed and there's no character left to choose, the humans lose.
  • Special new characters: original Baltar, Boomer, Apollo, Anders and alternate Zarek have extra rules when chosen after an execution (§22.1).

20. Ending the Game

The game ends immediately when:

  • A resource is at 0 at the end of any turn: Cylons win.
  • A centurion reaches the end of the Boarding Party track: Cylons win.
  • 6 Galactica locations are damaged at once: Cylons win.
  • A human is executed with no characters left: Cylons win.
  • The fleet jumps after reaching 10+ distance: don't draw a Destination. The humans win, as long as none of the conditions above has happened.

Then the Cylon Leader reveals any Motives they can, and wins or loses with a side (§17.2).

21. Miscellaneous Rules

21.1 Resources

  • Maximum 15, minimum 0. Values never go below 0.
  • A red resource icon means −1 of that resource.
  • When an effect raises or lowers "the highest" or "the lowest" resource and there's a tie, the current player chooses.
  • The red part of each dial means half or less.

21.2 Die rolls

Apply all modifiers, then cap the result to 1–8.

21.3 Timing

When several players want to act at once, the current player decides the order. If a card can't be played as a result (e.g. a second Strategic Planning), it returns to its owner's hand.

21.4 Component limits

  • Ships and tokens are limited to what's in the box. If a placement runs short, the current player chooses the order of placement and what isn't placed.
  • Empty decks reshuffle their discard piles: Skill, Quorum, Mutiny, Crisis, Super Crisis and Mission decks.

21.5 Destroying random civilian ships

When told to "draw a civilian ship to destroy" (e.g. Cain's Blind Jump, the Misjump destination):

  • Draw one from those not on the board.
  • If all are on the board, the current player picks one there.

21.6 Tips for a 7-player table

  • Appoint a Skill Supervisor (§9.2) and a Cylon Fleet tender who handles pursuit placements and dice.
  • Plan the order of your turn while others play theirs.
  • Keep this book's Quick Reference (Appendix E) at every seat.

    • If she uses Resolute to activate the Human Fleet while Infiltrating, she can't Infiltrate Galactica again.
    • If she activates Caprica while Infiltrating, she still gets an Activate Cylon Ships step.
    • If she activates Hub Destroyed while Infiltrating, she moves to the Cylon Fleet location and stops Infiltrating.
    • She can't use the Hangar Deck from the Brig.

  • "Caprica" Six (Leader): Intimate reads "Movement: Draw 1 Skill card at random from another player's hand. Then that player draws 1 card from the Skill deck of your choice (it may be outside his Skill set)."

  • Chief Tyrol: Blind Devotion can be used on a check affected by Investigative Committee, even after the cards are face up.

  • Ellen Tigh:

    • Politically Adroit and Support the People don't allow drawing outside the skill set.
    • Manipulative: if at the end of her turn Ellen no longer holds the title she took, it stays where it is. If the original holder can't take it back, it goes by succession, possibly to Ellen.

  • Felix Gaeta: FTL Operator and Helo's ECO Officer can both reroll the same die. The current player decides who goes first, and each still gets a chance.

  • Gaius Baltar (Political, original): receives 2 Loyalty cards at setup and adds 1 to the deck.

    • Chosen after an execution before the Sleeper phase: add 1 Not-a-Cylon card to the deck, shuffle, and deal him 1 card.
    • Chosen after the Sleeper phase: he can't use Cylon Detector.

  • Gaius Baltar (Support, alternate): no extra Loyalty card. The 3-token Broadcast rule is in §6.2.

  • Helena Cain:

    • Blind Jump overrides Assign Mission Specialist, which stays in play for the next jump.
    • Blind Jump can't be used at 7+ distance, so use it early.

  • Starbuck: Expert Pilot: if she starts her turn in a viper and moves to Galactica, Pegasus or Colonial One, she still gets two actions.

  • Helo (Military): a reroll with ECO Officer keeps the +2 from Strategic Planning.

  • Helo (Pilot):

    • If he gets a second Mutiny card while Stranded (a third, if he is the Mutineer), he discards down to 1 (or 2) and doesn't go to the Brig.
    • Chosen after an execution, he counts as Stranded on his next turn.

  • Laura Roslin: Terminal Illness: with fewer than 2 Skill cards in hand, she can't activate a location.

  • Apollo (Pilot):

    • Alert Viper Pilot works whenever a viper is placed on the board, including from Command. It doesn't work from the Brig, or on a viper someone else is piloting.
    • Headstrong applies to every forced discard, including the hand limit and crises, but not the card paid to change ships.
    • He may start in an assault raptor.
    • Chosen after an execution, he launches immediately in a viper, or starts on the Hangar Deck if none is available.

  • Kat:

    • Hot Shot can't be combined with Strategic Planning or other roll modifiers.
    • Stim Junkie reads "At the end of your Action step, if you are in the same location or space area you started your turn in, you are moved to Sickbay." It doesn't apply in the Brig.

  • Samuel T. Anders: chosen after an execution, he skips his next Receive Skills step.

  • Boomer:

    • Receives 2 Loyalty cards at the Sleeper phase and adds 1 to the deck.
    • Chosen after an execution before the Sleeper phase: shuffle 1 Not-a-Cylon card into the deck.
    • Chosen after the Sleeper phase: she starts in the Brig, and the player draws 1 extra Loyalty card.
    • If revealed as a Cylon before the Sleeper phase, she gets only 1 card there.

  • Doc Cottle: Treatment reads "Action: Choose another human player and draw 2 Skill cards from his Skill set. Then give him 2 Skill cards from your hand."

  • Tom Zarek (Political): Friends in Low Places affects Brig checks.

  • Tom Zarek (Military, alternate): chosen after an execution, he immediately draws a Mutiny card.

  • Tory Foster: Adaptable triggers whenever a Quorum card's action is resolved, including through the President's Office or Roslin's Skilled Politician. With Assign Mission Specialist it triggers when the card is given, not when it's used.

  • Hoshi:

    • Reluctant makes him discard a Skill card when he uses a Skill card's Movement ability (e.g. Critical Situation).
    • With Organized, the three locations must differ, but each time he activates Command, Communications or Weapons Control he may use Dutiful to discard a card and activate it again.

  • Tom Zarek (alternate): the Mutiny card from Disreputable at setup is subject to Necessary Step: draw 2, keep 1, put the other on the bottom.

  • Helo and alternate Apollo: Helo may use Moral Compass after Apollo's Choose a Different Path, but Apollo can't use Choose a Different Path after Moral Compass.

  • Athena: For Love works whenever a player discards a Skill card, except when discarding to the hand limit, when resolving a Super Crisis, or when discarding to pay for a character-sheet ability (e.g. Kat's Hot Shot, Hoshi's Dutiful, Anders's Star Player).

  • D'Anna: using Visions in the Brig doesn't make her discard down to 3.

  • Doral: Meticulous can't move him to the Resurrection Ship, because nobody moves voluntarily into a hazardous location.

  • Gaius Baltar (alternate): can't reveal a You Are a Cylon card outside the Brig, except when proving loyalty during an execution.

  • Doc Cottle: can use Engineering cards' non-Action text abilities.

22.2 Skill cards

  • Discarding "by choice": a discard counts as chosen unless it's random (e.g. Headstrong) or your whole hand (e.g. Tigh's Alcoholic). "Discard N" is always a choice, so discarding Dradis Contact or Bait that way draws a Mutiny card (once per turn).
  • Declare Emergency doesn't lower a partial pass number.
  • Executive Order:
    • Limit 1 per turn.
    • A Cylon targeted by one who reveals as the first action doesn't get the second action.
    • Cylons can't benefit from it.
  • Iron Will measures against the modified difficulty, and still costs 1 morale at total 0 or less. It doesn't change partial passes.
  • State of Emergency isn't targeted, so a Cylon can use it. If the current player plays it, it resolves fully even if that player is revealed or executed partway.
  • Negotiations doesn't stop Cylon ships being placed that turn, but it does stop centurions activating.
  • Change of Plans can be used after Political Prowess or Boomer's Mysterious Intuition has passed a check.
  • Consequence results are triggered by Treachery cards with the Skill Check Ability icon too.

22.3 Crises

  • Food Shortage: the President discards 2 and the current player discards 3. If they're the same player, discard 5.
  • Legendary Discovery, on a pass: place the card next to the Objective. It counts as 1 distance.
  • Standoff with Pegasus, on a pass: the current player may move 1 character from the Brig to any other Galactica location.
  • Unwelcome Faces, first option: the Admiral discards all Skill cards, then chooses a character to send to the Brig.
  • The Farm, on a fail: humans can no longer use miracle abilities; Cylon Leaders who aren't Infiltrating still can. Miracle tokens still pay for other things, such as Rebel Basestar locations.

22.4 Quorum cards

  • Consult the Oracle can't pick the Loyalty or Motive deck.
  • The President chooses when to discard Probation to use it.

22.4a Mutiny cards

  • Weapons Armed reads "Action: Destroy a raptor to gain an assault raptor. Then launch 2 raiders from each basestar and discard this card."

22.4b Civilian ships and the CAG

  • The CAG chooses the area only when an effect doesn't name one. If a card says "behind Galactica", that's where it goes. This includes the ships from Medal of Distinction.

22.5 Loyalty cards

  • Can Damage Galactica: for each of the 5 tokens, the current player chooses Galactica or Pegasus. The revealing Cylon then picks which 2 to resolve.

22.6 Destinations

  • Lion's Head Nebula: its +2 on the Jump Preparation track happens after the reset step.

Appendix A: Playing Without the Mutineer

A house option, not in use. Versions 1.0 and 1.1 of this book played this way.

  • Setup (§4 step 8): leave the Mutineer card in the box. The Loyalty deck is 2 Cylon + 11 Not-a-Cylon = 13 cards, plus the Baltar and Boomer extras as before. One card is still never dealt, so the odds of a Cylon card sitting out are about the same.
  • Ignore §15.5 and every line that mentions the Mutineer: Sleeper phase steps 3–4, revealing step 6, the Mutineer lines in §18 and §19.1, and the Mutiny draw in the Prepare for Jump step.
  • Everything else, including Mutiny cards and the second-card rule, is unchanged.

Appendix C: Allies for All Seasons (Undecided)

A fan variant (CR2.0 Appendix A4.5) that brings the Exodus Allies into non-Ionian games. Not in use; to be decided. The pros and cons are in the Decision Log.

Setup:

  • Put the Trauma tokens in an opaque container and remove the Disaster tokens.
  • Set up Allies as in Exodus: 3 Ally cards face up; place each Ally token on the location printed on its card. An Ally card showing a character a player is using is removed and replaced.
  • Mood Pool: each player draws 4 Trauma tokens, puts 1 face down into a shared Mood Pool (one per player) and keeps the rest.

Encountering an Ally:

  • At the end of your Movement step, if you share a location with an Ally token, draw one token from the Mood Pool and resolve that Ally's good result (Benevolent) or bad result (Antagonistic).
  • Keep that token, then put any one of your tokens back into the pool face down, and shuffle the pool.
  • Allies' draw and discard Trauma instructions use the container, never the pool.

Gaining Trauma:

  • Being sent to Sickbay or the Brig gives you 1 Trauma token from the container.
  • A Cylon revealed by execution and sent to the Resurrection Ship also gains 1.

Damaged Ally locations: if an Ally's location is damaged and there are revealed Cylons, the revealed Cylon with the most Trauma secretly looks at the pool and swaps one token with one of theirs.

The reckoning at 10 distance:

  • Everyone reveals their Trauma. Humans and hidden Cylons score +1 per Benevolent and −1 per Antagonistic; revealed Cylons score the reverse.
Score Result
−3 or less Executed (a revealed Cylon keeps their Super Crises)
−1 / −2 Discard 2 / 4 Skill cards
0 Nothing
+1 / +2 Draw 2 / 4 Skill cards (from any decks)
+3 or more Move any one resource dial up or down by 1
  • Then remove all Allies and Trauma from the game.
  • Suggested house tweak if used: hold the reckoning at 7 distance instead, so it happens mid-game and can't decide the ending.

Appendix D: What's New Since the Base Game

Teach in this order; about 30–40 minutes for base-game veterans.

  1. Earth and Missions (§14): 10 distance; the Demetrius Bridge replaces that turn's crisis.
  2. Cylon Fleet board (§12): Cylon ships never go away; the pursuit track and Auto Attack; the CAG; civilian ships stay across jumps; escorting.
  3. New ships: Viper Mark VIIs (start damaged, move 2), assault raptors, nukes aimed at an area.
  4. The Cylon Leader and Motives (§17): a known Cylon on an unknown side; Infiltration.
  5. Mutiny cards and the Mutineer (§18, §15.5): powerful actions; a second card sends you to the Brig; Treachery discards cause them. One player is the publicly known Mutineer, who draws them most turns and may hold two.
  6. Treachery and Reckless checks (§9.5–9.6): 2 Treachery in every Destiny deck.
  7. Miracle tokens (§6.2).
  8. Pegasus, the Airlock and execution (§19).
  9. Final Five (§15.2): looking at someone's loyalty is now risky.
  10. New overlays: the Resurrection Ship is hazardous; Caprica doesn't skip the jump step.

Appendix E: Quick Reference

Turn:

  1. Skills: draw your set; 1 card in Sickbay or on the Resurrection Ship; 3 for an Infiltrating Leader; 2 from different decks for Cylons.
  2. Move: 1 Skill card to change ships; no voluntary moves into hazardous locations.
  3. Action.
  4. Crisis: skip if revealed Cylon, in the Brig, or the Bridge was used this turn.
  5. Activate Cylon ships.
  6. Jump prep. On the Mutineer's turn, a jump icon also means they draw a Mutiny card.
  7. Discard to 10.

Skill check:

  1. Read.
  2. 2 Destiny cards.
  3. Play, starting left of the current player (Brig 1, Cylon 1, Infiltrator 2).
  4. Shuffle and split.
  5. Skill Check Abilities, then Consequence, then Reckless.
  6. Total.
  7. Result.

Jump:

  1. Vipers home (assault raptors may stay; civilians stay).
  2. Cylon ships to the Cylon Fleet board; clear basestar damage.
  3. Clear the Active Mission.
  4. Admiral draws 2 Destinations, keeps 1, bottoms 1.
  5. Resolve it.
  6. Objective check: 4 = Sleeper; 10 = the next jump wins.
  7. Reset the jump track.

Losing conditions:

  • A resource at 0 at the end of a turn.
  • A centurion at the end of the track.
  • 6 Galactica locations damaged at once.
  • No characters left after an execution.

Loyalty deck (7 players): 2 Cylon + 11 Not-a-Cylon + the Mutineer = 14 (+1 original Baltar, +1 Boomer).

Mutineer: revealed at once; draws another Loyalty card and 1 Mutiny card; gives up titles; draws a Mutiny card on each of their turns with a jump icon; Brig on the 3rd Mutiny card.

Brig:

  • Skip crises; play 1 card per check.
  • Escape check: 7, Politics + Tactics.
  • Out also via Presidential Pardon, Make a Deal, Dishonest Tactics or Rebuild Trust.
  • The Admiral and CAG lose their titles; the President keeps hers.
